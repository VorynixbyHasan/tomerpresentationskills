## 1 Reference intake
Inspect user references before planning the look. Separate approved inspiration from rejected output. Record layout ratios, title weight, accent usage, photography scale, translation hierarchy, and image treatment. Describe what is visible rather than calling it premium. Borrow the design grammar; do not copy people, logos, text, or private material. Never publish a user's photos or source presentation as reusable assets without explicit permission.

For Hasan's supplied deck, retain a white editorial canvas, oversized black headings, restrained lime highlights, small secondary English translations, and meaningful photo groups. Its content and faces belong to that presentation. Do not transfer them to another student. The schematic reference PNGs in assets show layout only, with neutral placeholders instead of real people.

## 2 Photo and font selection
Ask for the first slide's original portrait before designing it. Then request family, friends, hobbies, or city assets only as those slides approach. Confirm which person and image belongs to which caption. Offer unchanged rectangular placement as the default; ask before cutouts, monochrome, fades, or crops. Keep unedited originals available. Never regenerate a face as part of a full-slide image.

Show font specimens using the student's real heading and language. Offer a maximum of three pairings:
- Arial Bold / Arial: dependable editorial option with broad compatibility.
- Aptos Display Bold / Aptos: contemporary presentation option when installed.
- Georgia Bold / Arial: warm editorial option for a quieter story.
Use an installed equivalent only with disclosure. Test Turkish İ ı Ş ş Ğ ğ Ç ç Ö ö Ü ü, or the relevant script. For Arabic/Urdu, verify shaping and reading direction. Do not promise an installed font without checking.

## 3 Three layout directions
These are selectable starting points, not universal templates. Show the corresponding schematic before asking for a decision.

### A White editorial with lime
Reference: assets/reference-editorial.png
Background #FAFAF7, text #202321, accent #B6EE00. Roughly 60 percent light field, 30 percent text and photographic structure, 10 percent accent; treat this as a balance guide rather than literal pixel accounting. Title left, large portrait or photo group right. Use lime behind only the key word or short phrase. Translations use #737A74. No shadows or outer cards. Pair Arial Bold with Arial. This is the closest direction to Hasan's original deck.

### B Photo led charcoal
Reference: assets/reference-charcoal.png
Background #171A1C, text #F7F5EF, accent #E9B86D. Use one large documentary photo across about 55 percent of the slide and an aligned text column. Keep text on a solid field when the image is busy. Use a short human headline rather than decorative quotation text. Pair Aptos Display Bold with Aptos, falling back to Arial. Avoid glow, synthetic lighting, and gradients.

### C Warm magazine
Reference: assets/reference-magazine.png
Background #F4F0E7, text #252C29, accent #A04532. Use a large serif headline, one dominant image, and one smaller supporting photograph. Pair Georgia Bold with Arial. Use asymmetry with shared baselines and consistent margins. Avoid ornamental frames, fake paper textures, and random collage tilts.

## 4 Slide composition recipes
- About me: 40 percent text column, 52 percent portrait region, remaining space as gutter and margins. Use one name-led title, 3–5 short facts, and a small translation block. Ask for the portrait first.
- Family: one dominant group photograph plus up to two secondary images; factual relationship labels adjacent to the relevant photos. A photo-free version requires student choice. Never make up names to fill cards.
- Friends: image-led contact sheet with consistent gutters, varied photo sizes, and one brief personal sentence. Avoid biographies or captions the student did not provide.
- Hobbies: match every listed hobby to its actual image, using captions with a clear reading order. For seven hobbies, use an aligned 4 plus 3 image arrangement and concise text, or split into two slides if labels become too small.
- Personality: one expressive original portrait plus a short set of self-described traits; preserve nuance. Do not reduce the slide to three generic words centred across a blank background.
- City or country: choose images that support the spoken facts and use a coherent image grid. Separate verified factual text from personal opinion.
- Goal: make the actual ambition the headline, support it with a relevant student-supplied image or simple purposeful composition, and put thanks in a subordinate closing area. Never replace the goal with a generic thank-you headline.
- Rules: large left-aligned title and three numbered lines; no decorative images needed.

## 5 Production measurements
Use a 1920 × 1080 canvas or an equivalent 16:9 slide. Use 80–110 px outside margins, 40–60 px column gutters, and an 8 px spacing rhythm. Start with 88–112 px primary headings, 34–42 px body text, 26–30 px secondary translations, and 22–26 px captions. These correspond approximately to 44–56 pt headings and 17–21 pt body on a 13.333 inch wide PowerPoint. Increase size for large rooms or viewing distance; do not shrink to fit dense text. Prefer 3–5 short statements and split content when needed. Keep at least 4.5:1 contrast for ordinary text. Use a restrained radius only on photos when chosen; do not put every text item into a container.

Keep photos large enough to be recognised from the back of a classroom. Choose crop versus fit deliberately, protect faces, and never stretch. Use real text objects for accurate wording when tools support them. For image-based slides, place text reliably before export; avoid asking an image model to spell long paragraphs.

## 6 Rejection checks
Reject a preview when any answer is yes:
- Does it look like a SaaS dashboard or generic HTML card page?
- Are supplied photos missing or replaced by unrelated stock or generated art?
- Are important facts, faces, or captions changed?
- Is there a huge empty region without a deliberate visual role?
- Do the main headline and body appear similarly small?
- Are translations as prominent as the main language?
- Did the chosen font or accent change without permission?
- Does the goal slide say only thank you, or the family slide invent names?

Review at full size and as a small overview. Verify the slide at the intended presentation size, original-image placement, language glyphs, and actual PowerPoint export. Format validation alone is not a visual or behavioural test. Do not promise the same results across models; insist on actual previews and correct observed failures.
